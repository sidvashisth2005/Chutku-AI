# Recording guide: Chutku's voice

Thank you for lending Chutku your voice! This guide takes about **1 to 1.5 hours of recording in total**, and it can be spread over a few days.

Your recordings stay only on Siddhant's PC (in `D:\ChutkuVoice\`). They are never uploaded or shared, and the voice model made from them is used only by Chutku on that PC. You can ask for them to be deleted at any time.

---

## 1. Before you start (this matters more than anything else)

- **Quiet room.** No fan, AC, TV or music, and nobody else talking. A room with a bed, curtains or cupboards is better than an empty room with echo.
- **The same phone (or mic) every time, held at the same distance:** about one hand-span (15–20 cm) from your mouth, slightly to the side so your breath doesn't hit it.
- **The best quality setting.** On a phone voice recorder app, choose **WAV** or the highest quality (48 kHz or 44.1 kHz). Don't use WhatsApp to record.
- **Speak naturally, like Chutku talking to Siddhant:** friendly, warm, normal speed. Don't act or put on a voice; your normal voice is what we want.
- **Make a mistake? Just pause and say the line again.** Bad takes are cut out automatically.
- **Recording length:** about 5–10 minutes per file is perfect. Name the files in order, for example `part01.wav`, `part02.wav`.
- **Drink water and take breaks.** A tired voice sounds different, so it's better to record over a few short sessions.

## 2. The first recording: your permission (about 10 seconds)

Please say this, in your own words if you like:

> "Main, [your name], apni marzi se apni awaaz Chutku ke liye de rahi hoon, sirf Siddhant ke PC par use karne ke liye."

## 4. Free talk (about 30–40 minutes; this is just as important)

Just talk naturally, the way you normally speak, in a mix of Hindi and English. Some ideas:

- Tell Siddhant about your day, a funny memory, or a movie you liked.
- Read 5–10 minutes from any book, article or story you like (Hindi or English).
- Explain how to make your favourite dish, step by step.
- Pretend you're Chutku helping him: "Chalo, pehle ye file kholte hain, phir..."

Long and relaxed is better than perfect.

## 5. What to send, and what not to

| Source | Good for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ Voice recorder app, WAV, quiet room | **Training the voice** | This is the main material |
| 🆗 WhatsApp voice notes | A little extra variety | Compressed; only if clean and mostly your voice |
| ❌ Phone call recordings | Only a quick rough preview | Phone calls cut the sound to low quality and mix in the other voice |
| ❌ Videos with music or background noise | Not usable | |

**How much:** at least **30 minutes** of clean speech for a first version. **1–2 hours** gives a clearly better voice. More is welcome later; Chutku can be improved step by step.

**Where:** put the files in `D:\ChutkuVoice\raw\` on Siddhant's PC (any names are fine). Nothing else is needed. Chutku cuts the audio into sentences, writes down what was said, and drops bad parts by itself.

---

## For Siddhant: what happens next

1. **Preview.** A quick voice-copy model (XTTS-v2, which speaks Hindi and English) makes a sample from a few minutes of audio, so you both hear roughly how it will sound.
2. **Training.**
   - The recordings are cut into sentences and transcribed with Whisper; noisy or overlapping parts are dropped.
   - A Piper voice model is then trained on your RTX 3050 (several hours to about a day).
   - The finished voice runs on the processor, faster than real time, so it doesn't compete with the AI model for the graphics card.
3. **Blind listening test.** The new voice replaces the current one only if you both prefer it.
4. **Getting better over time.** New recordings can be added at any time. Retraining happens about weekly, and a new version is kept only if it sounds better in the listening test.
5. **Privacy.**
   - Recordings and the voice model stay in `D:\ChutkuVoice\` and `models\` on this PC. They are never committed to GitHub, because `models/` is ignored and `D:\ChutkuVoice` is outside the repository.
   - Delete them any time she asks.
